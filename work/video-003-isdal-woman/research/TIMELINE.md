## Dec 1970 – Feb 1971

| Date | Event | Src |
|------|-------|-----|
| ~2 Dec 1970 (three days after discovery) | Two suitcases found in left-luggage, Bergen railway station: 5×100 Deutsche Mark notes sewn into one lining (~US$137 in 1970), clothes, shoes, several wigs, makeup, eczema cream (prescription sticker scraped off), 135 NOK, Belgian/British/Swiss coins, maps, timetables, non-prescription glasses, sunglasses (partial fingerprints match), cosmetics, a notepad with coded entries. All identification removed | S1, S2 |
| Dec 1970 | Autopsy at Gades Institutt: death from combination of incapacitation by phenobarbital and carbon monoxide poisoning; 50–70 Fenemal sleeping pills in stomach (not fully absorbed); twelve more pills beside body; soot in lungs (alive while burning); unexplained bruise on right side of neck (fall or blow); no signs of illness; never pregnant; teeth/jaw removed for distinctive dental work (14 filled teeth, gold crowns, work of a kind seen in the Far East, Central/Southern Europe, South America; marked gap between upper front teeth) | S1, S2 |
| Dec 1970 – early 1971 | Notepad code partially cracked by police: entries are dates+places of travel (Paris, Stavanger, Bergen…), not espionage material | S1, S2 |
| Dec 1970 – 1971 | Hotel check-in forms analysed (handwriting): at least 8 aliases / fake passports; consistently claimed Belgian nationality; forms written in German or French; all street names false except Rue de la Madeleine; composite sketches + fingerprints circulated via Interpol; no identification | S1, S2 |
| 5 Feb 1971 | Catholic funeral (saints' names on forms) at Møllendal cemetery, Bergen; unmarked grave; zinc coffin to preserve remains; sixteen police officers attend; ceremony photographed in case relatives come forward; lilacs and tulips on the coffin. Case officially closed with verdict of probable suicide | S1, S2 |

## 1971 – 2015

| Date | Event | Src |
|------|-------|-----|
| 1970s–80s | Cold War espionage rumours persist in Bergen; Norwegian intelligence services review reports the woman had been seen observing military missile tests in western Norway; no clear conclusions in their reports | S2 |
| (undated) | Declassified Norwegian Armed Forces records: many of the woman's movements seem to correspond to top-secret Penguin missile trials; a fisherman reported seeing her near Penguin testing off Stavanger; Stavanger presence corroborated by the shoe sale | S1 |
| 1991 | Anonymous taxi driver claims that when he picked the woman up at her hotel, a second man joined her for the ride to Bergen railway station. The official taxi driver of 23 Nov was never located | S1 |
| 2005 | Bergen man (26 years old in 1970) tells Bergensavisen he saw a woman matching the sketch on Fløyen five days before the discovery, walking ahead of two men in coats; says a police contact told him to forget it; no record was made at the time | S1 |
| 2002 | Investigator Harald Osland's son Tore publishes a book on the case (Osland kept private police documents) | S1 |

## 2016 – present (modern forensics)

| Date | Event | Src |
|------|-------|-----|
| 2016 | NRK queries trigger case reopening. Prof. Inge Morild finds the preserved jawbone in Haukeland University Hospital's forensic archives (long believed destroyed); paraffin tissue samples of organs (lungs, heart, adrenal gland, ovaries) also held | S2 |
| 2016 | NRK commissions American forensic artist Stephen Missal: six alternative sketches, shown to people who had seen her | S1 |
| 19 May 2017 | BBC reports a "major breakthrough": isotope analysis underway on teeth from the preserved jaw | S1 (cited) |
| May 2017 | Stable isotope analysis (oxygen + strontium, Kripos & Univ. of Bergen): born c. 1930 ±4 years, in or near Nuremberg, Germany; moved as a child to France or the France–Germany border region; dental work points to a dentist in East Asia, Central Europe, Southern Europe or South America; handwriting analysis (earlier) suggested education in France or neighbouring country. Extended DNA profile published: European descent — making an Israeli-agent theory much less likely; Interpol black notice planned | S1, S2 |
| Apr 2018 | "Death in Ice Valley" podcast launches (NRK + BBC World Service); interviews with eyewitnesses and forensic scientists; suggests birthplace southern Germany or French–German border, born around 1930, probably raised in French-speaking Belgium | S1, S3 |
| Jun 2019 | BBC: podcast listeners supply new clues; geneticist Colleen Fitzpatrick (DNA Doe Project) offers genetic-genealogy testing of autopsied tissues | S1 |
| 2019 | mtDNA determined: haplogroup H24 — maternal line originating in South East Europe or South West Asia; an unidentified French national was registered on one of her flights to Norway (possible French passport) | S1 |
| Mar–Jun 2019 | Le Républicain Lorrain: a Forbach resident claims a summer-1970 relationship with the woman — polyglot with Balkan accent, looked younger than her age, refused personal details, received scheduled calls from abroad, kept wigs; photo published | S1 |
| 12 Jun 2023 | Neue Zürcher Zeitung article suggests possible connections with Swiss banker François Genoud and that Norwegian Intelligence Service (E-tjenesten) interfered with the local police investigation — sourced to a "professional fact-checker" | S1 |
| 2026 | Identity remains unestablished. She lies in the unmarked zinc-lined grave at Møllendal | S1 |
