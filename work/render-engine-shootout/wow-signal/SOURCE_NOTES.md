# SOURCE_NOTES — The Wow! Signal

All sources accessed **2026-10-18** (UTC+8 session). Retrieval method: direct web fetch.

## S1 — bigear.org, "Wow! The 30th Anniversary Report" (PRIMARY)
- URL: https://www.bigear.org/Wow30th/wow30th.htm
- Author: **Jerry R. Ehman** (discoverer of the signal), Big Ear website.
- Supports: detection date/time (Aug 15, 1977 ~11:16 p.m. EDT); discovery-on-review
  a few days later (prob. Aug 19); red-pen circling + "Wow!" margin note; 6EQUJ5
  decoding and intensity scale (0–9, A–Z = SNR 10–35; peak U = 30.5 ± 0.5 σ);
  72-s duration (6 × 12 s rows) and the 37-s HPBW misconception; rise-peak-fall
  shape; RA/Dec values (19h22m25s ±10s / −27°03′ ±20′, positive horn; 19h25m17s
  negative horn); one-horn-only fact; telescope description (Kraus, flat+paraboloid+
  ground plane, Earth-rotation scanning, no movable horns in 1977); Ohio Sky Survey
  (~20,000 sources, two record quasars); 1973 narrowband conversion (Dixon→Kraus,
  N50CH, IBM 1130, 50×10 kHz channels, character encoding); Guinness narrowband
  record; 1998 destruction (Ohio Wesleyan land, golf course + ~400 houses);
  hypotheses assessment (planets/asteroids/satellites/aircraft/spacecraft ruled out;
  debris-reflection improbable; lensing unlikely; scintillation possible; ETI not
  ruled out); Ehman's closing position ("open question… half-vast data… ETI might
  have sent… awaits additional signals").
- Nature: personal account by the discoverer, hosted on the observatory's own
  historical website. Treat as primary-memoir; cross-checked against Wikipedia.

## S2 — Wikipedia, "Wow! signal" (SECONDARY aggregator; follows cited literature)
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow!_signal
- Supports: 1959 Cocconi–Morrison proposal (Nature 159, 844); Sagittarius direction;
  follow-up history (Ehman 1977+; Gray META Oak Ridge 1987 & 1989; Shuch SETI League
  Green Bank 12-m Jul 1995; Gray VLA 1995 & 1996; Gray & Ellingsen Mount Pleasant 26-m
  Tasmania 1999, six 14-h observations; Breakthrough Listen GBT+ATA 2022-05-21);
  frequency accounting (Ehman 1998: 1420.4556 ± 0.005 MHz = 50 ± 5 kHz above
  1420.4058 MHz; Kraus 1994: 1420.3556 MHz typo story, LO ordered 1450.4056 delivered
  1450.5056; blueshift ≈ 10 km/s); comet hypothesis (Paris; 266P/Christensen,
  335P/Gibbs) and the three dismissal arguments; 72-s vs 37-s misconception;
  2024 Arecibo Wow! preprint summary.
- Notes: used for follow-up history and frequency accounting; underlying primary
  refs named in the article (Gray & Ellingsen 2002, ApJ; Shuch; Paris 2018 J. Wash.
  Acad. Sci.) are listed here as the trail for verification.

## S3 — arXiv:2408.08513, "Arecibo Wow! I" (PRIMARY preprint)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08513 (v2, 2024-09-11)
- Authors: Abel Méndez, Kevin Ortiz Ceballos, Jorge I. Zuluaga (Planetary
  Habitability Laboratory, UPR Arecibo).
- Supports: 2020 archived Arecibo drift scans finding narrowband (~10 kHz)
  hydrogen-line events from cold HI clouds but ~two orders of magnitude weaker than
  Wow!; hypothesis of transient excitation (magnetar flare / SGR) producing
  maser-like superradiance; framed as a new false-positive class for technosignature
  searches; Big Ear vs Arecibo comparison tables (Big Ear: 110 m cylindrical
  paraboloid, 50 channels, ~100 K receiver, operated 1963–1998, dismantled 1998);
  one-horn problem discussion; Ehman 2010 account reference.
- Nature: preprint (not peer-reviewed at the time of access) — script must say
  "proposed" / "researchers suggested".
