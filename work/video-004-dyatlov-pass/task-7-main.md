## 1. Documentary concept

### Recommended title

Dyatlov Pass: What Really Happened to the Nine Hikers?

### Alternative titles to test

* Nine Hikers Vanished Into a Russian Winter. The Evidence Remained.
* The Dyatlov Pass Mystery: What the Evidence Actually Says
* The Tent, the Mountain, and Nine Unanswered Deaths

### Thumbnail concept

Thumbnail text: `WHY DID THEY LEAVE?`

Keep the tent as the main focal point, the mountain behind it, and a small crimson
evidence marker. Avoid graphic imagery and avoid presenting an invented
reconstruction as an actual 1959 photograph.

## 2. Full research source pack

A. Original case files — primary evidence: scanned investigation documents, search
records, witness testimony, hikers' diaries, maps, autopsy reports.
https://dyatlovpass.com/case-files — useful research repository, but not an official
government-hosted archive; check translations and document context before quoting.

B. Original photographs and diaries:
https://dyatlovpass.com/search-photos and https://hibinafiles.ourproject.org/index-en.html
— check provenance of each item.

C. Peer-reviewed avalanche research (essential scientific source):
- 2021: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-020-00081-8 (Gaume & Puzrin, slab-
  avalanche mechanism under the site's particular conditions)
- 2022 follow-up: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-022-00393-x
- Present as a scientifically testable explanation; NOT as proof that every detail
  of the incident is conclusively resolved.

D. Image and map archive: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Dyatlov_Pass_incident
(one documented example: photograph of the tent found 26 Feb 1959; check file page
provenance and rights).

## 3. Image sources: what the agent should download

Build a local asset manifest: direct file URL, creator, date, source page, licence,
intended scene.

| Visual asset | Best source | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Recovered tent photograph | Wikimedia file page | Opening hook and evidence reveal |
| Expedition group photographs | 1959 photo archive | Introduce hikers, chronology |
| Original diaries / investigation documents | Case-file archive | Document close-ups, timelines |
| Route maps and location diagrams | Wikimedia incident category | Geographic explanation |
| Snow/avalanche scientific illustrations | 2021 research paper | Physical hypothesis |
| Contemporary mountain establishing shots | Wikimedia Commons | Atmosphere, transitions |

**Copyright rule**: a public archive does not mean every image on it is free for
commercial reuse. Verify the individual file's licence, attribution requirements,
and any restrictions before including it in a monetized documentary. Do not
download copyrighted images from news sites and assume that credit alone makes
reuse lawful.

## 4. The 15–18 minute narrative plan

- **0:00 Hook — The abandoned tent.** Begin with the documented discovery. Establish
  the severe winter conditions and the question that drives the film. Avoid inventing
  dialogue or showing fictional events as archival footage.
- **2:00 Context — Nine experienced hikers.** Expedition, route, equipment, weather,
  last documented diary entries.
- **5:00 Evidence — What the searchers found.** Reconstruct the discovery using
  photographs, case documents, maps, verified autopsy findings. Explain what is
  documented and what remains uncertain.
- **8:00 Theories — The competing explanations.** Compare natural hazards, the
  avalanche hypothesis, exposure, injuries, alternative theories against the evidence.
  Do not give unsupported claims equal weight with peer-reviewed analysis.
- **12:00 Science — Can an avalanche explain the evidence?** The 2021 model in
  accessible language, its assumptions, the 2022 follow-up and field observations,
  and its limitations.
- **15:00 Conclusion — Mystery versus evidence.** Which parts have plausible
  scientific explanations, which details cannot be established with certainty. End
  with a strong, honest conclusion.

Can modern science explain the evidence that transformed the deaths of nine hikers
into one of history's most enduring mysteries?

1. The expedition and its last documented movements.
2. The discovery of the tent and the search operation.
3. The physical evidence and the autopsy findings, without gratuitous or graphic imagery.

6. What remains uncertain and why no dramatic theory should be treated as established fact.

rights are verified. Use animated maps, terrain diagrams, timelines, and clearly
labelled reconstructions for the rest.

quotations, documents, weather records, or forensic findings.

Maintain the channel's black, archival beige, and crimson visual identity, with
restrained cinematic sound design and scene-specific visuals.
